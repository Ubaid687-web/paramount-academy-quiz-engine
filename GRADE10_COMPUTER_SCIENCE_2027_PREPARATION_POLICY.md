# Grade 10 Computer Science: Chronological Examination-Preparation Policy

## Purpose and Evidence Boundary

This policy improves **exam preparation**, not paper prediction. The active user-supplied Grade 10 Computer Science textbook remains the sole authority for every factual question, correct answer, marking point, and solution explanation. The supplied model and annual-paper records are used only to observe concise command forms, section presentation, cognitive demand, and the way a textbook concept can be asked without being copied verbatim.

> A past-paper question is neither a future-paper forecast nor an answer source. It may inform a new task form only when the new item is supported by a different, explicit textbook concept or learning objective.

## What the Chronology Shows

The supplied papers show that exam preparation cannot stop at memorising a definition, a heading, or a textbook exercise question. A textbook concept may be asked through a direct term, a purpose, a reason, a comparison, a representation, a short trace, a code correction, a small process, or a familiar application. The paper is concise, but the student still has to recognise which textbook relationship is being tested.

| Section | Retained task-form evidence | Student preparation response |
|---|---|---|
| **A: MCQs** | Direct term/representation prompts appear for algorithms, flowchart shapes, HTML, system components, digital-divide measures, machine learning, blockchain, NLP, and data representation. | Memorise the textbook term **with its function, relation, or identifying feature**. Practise one compact stem with answer-shaped options, not a paragraph. |
| **B: short questions** | The corpus includes `define … with an example`, `what is … and why`, `what is meant by`, `name`, `why is … important`, code correction, and brief syntax/process forms. | Practise a two-mark answer as **term or statement → one textbook reason/purpose/example**. The student should learn how to make one or two correct sentences, not only repeat a heading. |
| **C: long questions** | The corpus includes trace tables, flowcharts/representations, code or correction work, process/role explanations, comparison, and application of a textbook concept. | Practise a five-mark answer as **opening statement → 3–5 textbook points/steps → example, diagram, trace, or comparison when the textbook supports it**. |

## Controlled Preparation Method

For every selected chapter, the system should prepare students through a compact sequence. First, it tests the **term and its meaning**. Next, it tests the **purpose, reason, relationship, or contrast**. It then uses a small textbook-supported representation, trace, step, or familiar situation where that is appropriate. This progression lets a student who normally memorises an exercise recognise the same underlying concept when the exam changes the wording.

For example, the system may use the textbook to prepare a student to recognise a system-software role, explain why a binary system is used, make a small algorithm trace, or describe how a process works. It must not reuse a supplied paper question, fabricate a fact, or keep returning to number conversion while other selected concepts such as operating-system functions, process management, device management, threads, algorithms, programming, data, applications, impacts, digital literacy, or entrepreneurship remain available.

## Generation Controls Now Applied

| Control | Enforced behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Chronological wording** | Generation uses concise one-focus command forms observed in the supplied papers, such as identify, define, state a purpose/reason, compare, trace, correct, represent, or apply. |
| **Textbook-wide coverage** | The selected textbook scope is broken into visible coverage slots. Each printable OR pair receives one slot, and repeated topic labels/objectives are rejected. |
| **Exercise-to-transfer** | End-of-chapter questions are not copied or lightly reworded. A new item uses a neighbouring textbook-supported relationship, changed value/representation, or familiar use. |
| **Accessible answer practice** | Questions and solution keys retain textbook terms but prompt a student to give a purpose, reason, relation, step, comparison point, or result in plain exam English. |
| **No conversion dominance** | A second conversion, calculation, or code family is not allowed while another selected textbook concept is available for coverage. |
| **No prediction claim** | Recent 2026 evidence expands observed forms and presentation; it does not increase a topic's claimed probability of appearing in 2027. |

## What Remains Deliberately Unknown

The chronological evidence does not prove that a specific topic will repeat, that a supplied 2025 page group is a supply/improvement paper, or that a 2026 evening long question carries a particular individual mark. Those gaps stay visible in the evidence record and are never filled by general knowledge or by inference from another subject.
