13.3 Lipids
A lipid is a component of plant or animal tissue that is insoluble in water, but soluble in
solvents of low polarity such as ether, hexane, benzene and carbon tetrachloride. The
primary function of lipids in the body is long-term energy storage.
Lipids include:
a) Fats and oils
b) Cholesterol
c) Sex hormones
d) Components of the cell membrane called phospholipids
e) Some vitamins (A, D, E and K)
Fats and oils are referred to as simple lipids. They are esters of fatty acids with a trihydroxy
alcohol, glycerol. For this reason, they are called glyceryl esters or glycerides. Fatty acids
are long-chain carboxylic acids. They are the building blocks of lipids.
