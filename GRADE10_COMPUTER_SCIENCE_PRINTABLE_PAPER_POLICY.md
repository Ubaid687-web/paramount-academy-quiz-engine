# Grade 10 Computer Science Printable-Paper Policy

## What the Reported Draft Revealed

The reported short-question PDF was not an acceptable FBISE-style preparation paper. It presented ten numbered pairs as `Q1`–`Q10`, counted both alternatives as marks, showed an invented 60-minute recommendation, and repeated Unit 1 conversion and two’s-complement outcomes. These are generator and renderer failures, not a conclusion about the textbook or the student.

| Observed failure | Verified cause | Corrected policy |
|---|---|---|
| Ten pairs instead of the verified eleven-part Section B shape | The builder and route imposed a maximum of ten OR pairs, even though the active 2024/2025 profiles agree on eleven. | The controlled limit is now eleven; choosing a short paper prefills the active reviewed count of eleven. |
| `40 marks` for ten two-mark OR pairs | The renderer added marks for both alternatives even though a student attempts one. | Total marks now count one attemptable alternative from each pair. Eleven two-mark parts display as 22 marks. |
| `Suggested time: 60 minutes` | The generator used a generic `pairCount × 6` formula, not a verified paper-time source. | A standalone Section B/C draft now has no invented time. It displays a time only if a verified value is available. |
| `Q1`–`Q10` Section B layout | The renderer applied generic question numbering rather than the observed written-paper form. | An exact 11 × 2 short paper renders `SECTION B`, `Q.2`, and parts `(i)`–`(xi)` with matched `OR` alternatives. |
| Repeated conversion questions | Each small generation batch had no whole-paper topic plan or duplicate-topic control. | Each OR pair must declare one internal topic label and one shared objective/reference; topic labels and objectives cannot repeat across the paper. |

## Topic Balance and Student-Answer Practice

The purpose is not to make students copy headings, paragraphs, or exercise questions. A good generated paper begins with the textbook’s exact terminology and then asks for one examinable relationship: a purpose, condition, comparison, step, cause, result, representation, or familiar application. The solution key uses short textbook-faithful points in accessible English so students can learn how to express an answer in an exam.

| Requirement | Generator control |
|---|---|
| Whole-paper coverage | The prompt plans the complete paper first and spreads OR pairs across distinct textbook topics available in the chosen source scope. |
| No conversion-heavy paper | A conversion, calculation, definition, or process cannot occupy more than one topic family when other verified topics are available. |
| Non-repeating OR pairs | Both alternatives share a source reference and learning objective; another pair cannot reuse that objective or internal topic label. |
| Textbook-to-paper transfer | An exercise supports practice, but the generator must change the tested relationship, value, condition, representation, or familiar application. It cannot copy or lightly paraphrase an exercise. |
| Accessible exam English | Questions request a clear purpose, relationship, condition, comparison, step, or result in familiar textbook terms—not an English-heavy narrative or a copied paragraph. |
| Scope safety | If a chosen chapter or block lacks enough distinct verified topics for the requested pair count, the paper fails safely and asks for a wider approved scope or fewer pairs. |
