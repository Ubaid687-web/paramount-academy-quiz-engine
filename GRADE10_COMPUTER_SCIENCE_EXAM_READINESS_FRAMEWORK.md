# Grade 10 Computer Science Exam-Readiness Analysis Framework

## Purpose and Sequence

This framework governs the Grade 10 analysis that must be completed before any Grade 9 expansion or new front-end work. Its purpose is to improve preparation for the 2027 FBISE examination through **textbook-faithful memorisation priorities** and **applied-knowledge practice**, not through blind question generation.

## Evidence Classes

| Evidence class | Stored or expected material | Permitted use | Not permitted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Factual authority | Active Grade 10 Computer Science textbook and its reviewed units/exercises | Verify concepts, definitions, code, diagrams, terminology, and every answer claim | Adding unstated facts, modern examples, or inferred syllabus content |
| Exercise evidence | Textbook worked examples and exercises within the approved source | Identify the direct practice baseline and likely prerequisite skills | Treating an exercise question as a template to copy |
| Pattern evidence | Model paper, 2024 paper, 2025 paper, and future 2026 annual paper | Analyse sections, marks, timing, wording, command verbs, cognitive demand, OR structure, and recurring exam forms | Supplying answer facts or copying/paraphrasing questions |
| Analysis notes | The user’s re-uploaded analysis document | Form hypotheses about weaknesses, repetition, and preparation priorities | Treating statements as verified exam or textbook facts |

## Analysis Questions

For each approved Grade 10 unit, the analysis will distinguish **direct textbook memorisation** from the additional thinking expected in papers.

| Dimension | Required analysis |
|---|---|
| Memorisation baseline | Terms, definitions, syntax, rules, diagrams, formulae, tables, and exercise procedures that must be recalled accurately from the textbook |
| Understanding transformation | How papers ask students to explain a purpose, differentiate concepts, trace a result, correct an error, or relate two verified textbook ideas |
| Applied-knowledge transformation | How papers use a simple scenario, calculation, code fragment, diagram, data/table, or familiar problem to require a supported textbook concept rather than outside knowledge |
| Wording control | Which concise command verbs and familiar textbook terms occur across supplied papers; wording must test Computer Science, not difficult English |
| Recurrence signal | A topic is marked as recurring only where it appears in more than one supplied paper or is supported by the model-paper design; it is never a prediction or guarantee |
| Exercise-to-exam gap | The extra reasoning step between an existing exercise and an observed paper form, expressed without copying either question |

## Deliverable Structure

The Grade 10 readiness output will contain a unit-by-unit map with: (1) source references to textbook sections/exercises, (2) memorisation priorities, (3) understanding transformations, (4) applied-knowledge transformations, (5) safe practice forms, and (6) evidence notes identifying the supporting papers. It will label all conclusions as evidence-backed guidance, not a promise of future FBISE questions.

> **Non-negotiable rule:** Grade 9 materials and patterns remain separate. No Grade 9 source, structure, or question assumption may be mixed into this Grade 10 analysis.

## Current Evidence Inventory

The current Grade 10 Computer Science pattern inventory contains the supplied model paper (Curriculum 2022–23) and annual papers for 2024 and 2025. The 2026 annual paper and the user’s re-uploaded analysis document are required before the final recurrence and applied-knowledge map is completed.
