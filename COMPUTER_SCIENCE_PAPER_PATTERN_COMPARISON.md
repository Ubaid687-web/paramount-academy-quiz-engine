## Wording and Cognitive Signals

The three sources consistently use short command wording. Common observed prompt forms include **define**, **state**, **write down**, **enlist**, **differentiate**, **evaluate**, **identify**, **calculate**, **convert**, **draw**, **compare**, **explain**, and **write a program/code**. Generated wording should remain direct, use familiar textbook terms, and avoid unnecessarily difficult English.

The annual papers show a broad progression. Section A primarily checks accurate terms, syntax, symbols, simple conversion, and code-output recognition. Section B mixes knowledge and understanding through definitions, purposes, differences, corrections, traces, and concise code or logic tasks. Section C contains more extended understanding and application, including algorithms, flowcharts, programs, comparisons, Boolean simplification/circuits, and HTML tasks. The model paper additionally shows scenario, responsibility, data-analysis, and technology-context prompts; these may guide variety only when the selected textbook chapter supports them.

> **Operational rule:** The active Grade 10 Computer Science textbook remains the sole factual boundary. The three reviewed paper profiles guide structure, command wording, marks, cognitive balance, and OR construction only. No past-paper or model-paper question is copied, paraphrased, or treated as an answer source.
