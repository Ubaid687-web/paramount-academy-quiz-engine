# Cross-Subject Textbook-to-Paper Analysis Method

## Purpose

The uploaded **SSC-I Physics** document is used only as a method reference. It does **not** provide Grade 10 Computer Science facts, topic priorities, marks, timing, paper layout, SLOs, or answer evidence.

## Transferable Method

| Method step | Grade 10 Computer Science application | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Identify the core textbook concept | Name the exact Computer Science concept, syntax rule, process, algorithm, diagram, table, or procedure being assessed | Approved Grade 10 Computer Science textbook unit/exercise |
| Compare direct practice with paper wording | Identify the reasoning step added when a paper turns a direct exercise into a trace, correction, comparison, scenario, code fragment, or applied task | Same textbook item plus a supplied Computer Science model/annual-paper form |
| Measure the deviation | Describe the change in wording or context while confirming that the underlying answer remains fully supported by the textbook | Textbook and Computer Science paper evidence together |
| Control language | Replace unfamiliar or unnecessarily difficult English with familiar Computer Science textbook terms; difficulty must come from the concept or reasoning step | Grade 10 Computer Science textbook register |
| Classify the demand | Label each proposed preparation item as Knowledge, Understanding, or Application, then audit the overall balance | Actual Grade 10 Computer Science papers and the selected textbook scope |
| Teach transferable recognition | Group several safe forms under one core concept so students learn to recognise a familiar concept inside an unfamiliar exam question | Verified Computer Science concept-to-paper mappings |
| Preserve uncertainty | Flag an item for review when the textbook, exercise, or Computer Science papers do not support the proposed conclusion | Missing or conflicting evidence |

## Explicit Exclusions

The following are excluded from Grade 10 Computer Science analysis unless independently supported by its own sources: Physics examples, Physics concepts, Grade IX assumptions, the reference document’s 12-MCQ/3-mark format statements, its formula-sheet convention, and any claim about a specific assessment framework.

> **Operational rule:** For Grade 10 Computer Science, this document supplies the *analysis question*—"what is the controlled step from the textbook exercise to the exam form?"—not the answer. Every actual conclusion must cite the Grade 10 Computer Science textbook and its supplied model/annual papers.
